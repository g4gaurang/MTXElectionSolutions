Why election offices are modernizing

Election administration depends on coordinated data, people, and deadlines.

Election officials manage voter records, election setup, ballots, polling locations, workers, candidate filings, disclosures, and public reporting across systems with different roles.

Industry pressure 01

Voter records across sources

Registration activity, address changes, eligibility information, and voting history may arrive through separate channels.

MTX Gov response

Configurable intake, matching, review, history, and integrations support voter record maintenance.

Industry pressure 02

Complex election preparation

Districts, contests, candidates, ballot styles, deadlines, and jurisdiction rules must align for each election.

MTX Gov response

Election setup workflows connect calendars, districts, contests, candidates, ballots, and approval checkpoints.

Industry pressure 03

Polling readiness at scale

Locations, equipment, supplies, accessibility, staffing, training, and contingency tasks require coordinated preparation.

MTX Gov response

Readiness workspaces bring polling places, resources, workers, assignments, and open tasks into view.

Industry pressure 04

Election Day issue response

Field questions, equipment incidents, supply needs, and staffing changes can require time-sensitive coordination.

MTX Gov response

Service requests, incident triage, communications, assignments, and status updates support field operations.

Industry pressure 05

Filing and disclosure deadlines

Candidates and committees may need guidance for registration, periodic filings, amendments, and public disclosure.

MTX Gov response

Guided filings, schedules, validation, staff review, notices, and searchable disclosure support configured programs.

Industry pressure 06

Results and public communication

Unofficial reporting, canvass, audit, and certification are distinct stages that require clear status communication.

MTX Gov response

Reporting workflows distinguish unofficial results from canvass and certification controlled by election officials.

Election context is informed by federal guidance on administration, accessibility, security, audit trails, canvass, and certification. Read EAC Election Management Guidelines Read the NIST Election Infrastructure Profile

Architecture & integration model

Coordinate election work across designated systems.

MTX Gov Election Management can be delivered on Microsoft, Salesforce, or AWS technology foundations based on agency architecture and delivery needs. It connects digital services and operational workflows through modular APIs while designated election systems retain their authoritative roles.

Election officials retain statutory authority. The product supports administration and reporting workflows; it does not certify results or replace certified voting and tabulation systems.

Staged adoption roadmap

Begin with priority election workflows, then extend the product foundation.

Phased configuration lets election offices align adoption with statutory calendars, operational readiness, security, integrations, and funding.

Stage 1 · Establish

Configure the election foundation

Set up voter services, election definitions, roles, access, core integrations, reporting, security, accessibility, testing, and training.

Stage 2 · Extend

Add operational workflows

Extend ballots, polling places, workers, field support, candidate filings, campaign finance, and public reporting through shared patterns.

Stage 3 · Assist

Introduce governed AI assistance

Support voter guidance, document classification and extraction, filing completeness, issue triage, staff summaries, suggested correspondence, and anomaly identification with privacy controls, audit history, and staff review.

AI is assistive. Authorized election officials retain decision, canvass, certification, and reporting authority. AI does not determine voter eligibility, ballot access, vote totals, or election outcomes.
